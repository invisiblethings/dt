30-second chord taste

Play your first chord: C major

Press C, then E, then G, then hear all three together. That one hand shape is the start of the chord-first idea: learn a few shapes and you can play along with real songs long before you can read a page of notes.

The Starter Pack: What You Actually Need

You don’t need an acoustic piano to start. An 88-key digital piano with weighted keys, like a Yamaha P-145 or Casio Privia, covers everything on this list, and it’s what I recommend you start on. For app-based courses you’ll also want a smartphone or tablet, a stable internet connection, and if you’re eyeing Piano Marvel or Playground Sessions specifically, a USB or MIDI cable to connect your keyboard to your computer or device.

A good method book next to the screen helps too: my list of the best piano books for adult beginners has the ones I use with students. And if you’d rather piece together your own path, start with my guide on how to teach yourself piano.

How to Choose the Right One

Six things actually matter here, and pricing isn’t the first one. The course-finder quiz at the top asks about each of them, if you’d rather tap than read.

What do you actually want to play? Pop songs, classical pieces, or improvisation each point toward a different platform. Be honest about this before you look at price.

Match your learning style. Video-first learners tend to like Pianote. If gamification keeps you coming back, Simply Piano fits. If you want the app correcting you in real time rather than a teacher answering your questions later, Skoove does that well.

Think about your schedule. Pianoforall is a one-time payment, so there’s no pressure to “keep up” with a subscription. Mobile-first apps like Flowkey suit people who practice in short, scattered windows rather than one long session.

Balance cost against value. A one-time $49 purchase and a $30-a-month subscription solve different problems even when the curriculum overlaps. Two months of a $30 membership like Musora’s costs more than Pianoforall outright. The 12-month cost race in the quiz result puts every course side by side.

Don’t ignore community. Piano Marvel’s leaderboards and challenges keep some students motivated in a way solo practice never will. If you know you need external accountability, weight this higher than curriculum depth.

Always try before you commit. Almost every platform here has a free tier, a free trial or a money-back guarantee, and Pianoforall now offers both a 7-day free trial and a 60-day guarantee; Playground Sessions lays out its trial terms only after you sign up. Use that window to test whether the feedback mechanism (mic versus MIDI versus a real teacher) actually works with your setup before you pay for a year. Frequently Asked Questions

Are online piano lessons actually effective?

For most beginners, yes, they work comparably to in-person instruction. The one habit worth building regardless of platform: record yourself occasionally. It’s the fastest way to catch posture and hand-position issues that a screen won’t flag.

What equipment do I need for online piano lessons?

I recommend an 88-key digital piano with weighted keys, because it has the full range and the feel of a real piano (see my best beginner pianos). You don’t need an acoustic piano, and most students shouldn’t buy one until they know they’re sticking with it.

How much do online piano lessons cost?

Monthly plans on this list run from $16.49 (Flowkey Classic) to $49 (Piano by Pictures, after its free first month). Yearly plans cost less per month: Piano Marvel is $129.99 a year, Skoove and Flowkey Premium are $149.99, and Pianote, now a Musora membership, is $279. Pianoforall is the outlier: a 7-day free trial, then a $49 one-time fee with no recurring cost. Simply Piano is $169.90 a year ($17.90 a month) and Playground Sessions is $149.99 a year ($19.99 a month). Prices checked October 12, 2026.

Which online piano lessons are best for complete beginners?

Pianoforall gets you playing songs fastest. Simply Piano works well if you need app-style motivation to keep showing up, and it’s the one I suggest for kids. Piano by Pictures suits beginners who want to avoid reading traditional sheet music at first.

How long until I can actually play something?

With 20 minutes of daily practice, expect simple melodies within 2 to 3 weeks and basic two-hand songs within 2 to 3 months. That timeline holds across most of the platforms above; the differences show up more in what you’re playing than how fast.

Am I too old to start?

No. Adults often progress faster than kids in the first few months because practice is intentional rather than forced. Age isn’t the variable that predicts whether you stick with it. Consistency is.
