The Starter Pack: What You Actually Need You don’t need an acoustic piano to start. A digital piano with 61 or more weighted keys , like a Yamaha P-145 or Casio Privia, covers everything on this list. For app-based courses you’ll also want a smartphone or tablet, a stable internet connection, and if you’re eyeing Piano Marvel or Playground Sessions specifically, a MIDI cable to connect your keyboard to your computer or device. How to Choose the Right One Six things actually matter here, and pricing isn’t the first one. What do you actually want to play? Pop songs, classical pieces, or improvisation each point toward a different platform. Be honest about this before you look at price. Match your learning style. Video-first learners tend to like Pianote. If gamification keeps you coming back, Simply Piano fits. If you want the app correcting you in real time rather than judging your video later, Skoove does that well. Think about your schedule. Pianoforall gives you lifetime access to everything, so there’s no pressure to “keep up” with a subscription. Mobile-first apps like Flowkey suit people who practice in short, scattered windows rather than one long session. Balance cost against value. A one-time $49 purchase and a $24.99/month subscription solve different problems even when the curriculum overlaps. Six months of Playground Sessions costs more than Pianoforall outright. Don’t ignore community. Piano Marvel’s leaderboards and challenges keep some students motivated in a way solo practice never will. If you know you need external accountability, weight this higher than curriculum depth. Always try before you commit. Every platform on this list offers some kind of trial. Use it to test whether the feedback mechanism (mic versus MIDI versus a real teacher) actually works with your setup before you pay for a year. Final Thoughts There’s no single best answer here, and I’d be lying if I gave you one. What decides whether you’re still playing in a year isn’t which platform ranks first on this page. It’s whether the platform matches how you actually learn. Pick based on that, practice consistently, and any of these eight will get you further than the piano sitting untouched in the corner right now. Frequently Asked Questions Are online piano lessons actually effective? For most beginners, yes, they work comparably to in-person instruction. The one habit worth building regardless of platform: record yourself occasionally. It’s the fastest way to catch posture and hand-position issues that a screen won’t flag. What equipment do I need for online piano lessons? A digital keyboard with 61 or more keys is enough to start. You don’t need an acoustic piano, and most students shouldn’t buy one until they know they’re sticking with it. How much do online piano lessons cost? Monthly subscriptions on this list run $12.99 to $24.99. Pianoforall is the outlier at a $49 one-time fee with no recurring cost. Which online piano lessons are best for complete beginners? Pianoforall gets you playing songs fastest. Simply Piano works well if you need the app-based motivation to keep showing up. Piano by Pictures suits beginners who want to avoid reading traditional sheet music at first. How long until I can actually play something? With 20 minutes of daily practice, expect simple melodies within 2 to 3 weeks and basic two-hand songs within 2 to 3 months. That timeline holds across most of the platforms above, the differences show up more in what you’re playing than how fast. Am I too old to start? No. Adults often progress faster than kids in the first few months because practice is intentional rather than forced. Age isn’t the variable that predicts whether you stick with it. Consistency is.
