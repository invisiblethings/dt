How Pianoers should be built, and what to do next

Google trusts a site that covers its whole subject and connects it well. Your subject is "starting piano": what to buy and how to learn. This page shows the plan in five steps. Tick tasks as you finish them, and your progress is saved.

The idea in 30 seconds

Three things to remember

1. One subject, covered fully

Every post should help someone start playing piano. Posts that don't, like a viral-kid profile, weaken the site's focus.

2. Money pages and supporting pages

Two groups earn money: digital pianos and online courses. Four groups exist to build trust: learning, care, the instrument, and pianists.

3. Supporting pages link to money pages

Each supporting post sends readers on to a money page with a natural sentence and link. That passes trust where it pays.

Writing

Rules for every post

One topic per page

The URL, title, H1 and first sentence all name the same thing.

Answer first

The first sentence under each heading gives the answer. Explain after.

Headings in the reader's order

What it is, who it's for, how it works, price, pros and cons, alternatives, verdict.

Something only you have

Your test notes, photos, a recording or a real student timeline. This is what beats big sites.

Links inside the text

3 to 8 per post, with descriptive words ("our Roland FP-10 review", never "here"). Link to the group's main post and 1 or 2 neighbours.

Same words everywhere

"Digital piano" means 88 weighted keys, "keyboard" means the rest. Always recommend 88 weighted keys.

Real updates only

Change the "Updated" date only when prices or facts change.

Check before deleting

Any post with clicks in Search Console over the last 12 months gets rewritten, not removed.

What it is→Who it's for→How lessons work→Levels→Songs and genres→Devices→Price→Trial and refund→Liked→Missing→vs alternatives→Verdict

Every course review uses the sections above, in this order. Readers can compare, and Google learns the pattern.
