# Helpful Facebook posts (no links)

Ten cheat-sheet posts with images, plus 12 text posts that get people talking. None of them link out, because posts without links usually reach more people on Facebook, and cheat sheets are what people save and share with friends who are learning.

**How to use them**
- **Cheat sheets:** post 1–2 a week, mixed with the text posts. Upload the image from `assets/` and paste the caption as is.
- **Reply to comments quickly.** It's the biggest single boost to reach.
- **Asking people to share is fine when it's natural** ("send this to someone who's learning"). Avoid "Like if…/Share if…" bait, which Facebook demotes.
- If someone asks where to learn more, reply with the relevant Pianoers link **in a comment**. That keeps the post itself link-free.

## Cheat-sheet posts (with images)

### 1. `tip-find-any-note.jpg`
> The fastest way to stop feeling lost on the keyboard 🎹
>
> Look for the black keys. They come in groups of 2 and 3.
> 👉 C is the white key just left of the 2 black keys.
> 👉 F is the white key just left of the 3 black keys.
>
> From C, the white keys simply go C D E F G A B, then start again. Find C, and you can find every note.
>
> Save this, and send it to someone who's just bought a keyboard.

### 2. `tip-finger-numbers.jpg`
> Those little numbers next to the notes? They tell you which finger to use. 🖐️
>
> 1 = thumb, 2 = index, 3 = middle, 4 = ring, 5 = pinky.
>
> Both thumbs are 1, so your hands are mirror images: "5" is the left pinky in the left hand and the right pinky in the right hand.
>
> Good fingering feels slow at first, then makes everything easier. Trust the numbers.

### 3. `tip-major-minor-chords.jpg`
> You don't need to memorise 24 chords. You need one rule. 🎶
>
> Major chord: start on any key, count up 4 keys, then 3 more (count black keys too).
> C → E → G = C major
>
> Minor chord: swap the gaps. 3 keys, then 4.
> C → E♭ → G = C minor
>
> That's it. Try it starting on D, then on F. Tell me in the comments which chord surprised you.

### 4. `tip-four-chords.jpg`
> Four chords. Hundreds of songs. 🎹
>
> C – G – Am – F
>
> Play each one for 4 beats, in that order, and loop it. You'll start hearing songs you know: it's the chord pattern musicians call I–V–vi–IV.
>
> Once it feels comfortable, try singing a melody you know over the top.
>
> Which song did you hear first? 👇

### 5. `tip-major-scale-formula.jpg`
> Every major scale follows the same pattern:
> W W H W W W H
>
> W = whole step (skip one key, black or white)
> H = half step (the very next key)
>
> Start on C and you'll play only white keys. Start on G, and the pattern makes you play one black key (F♯). Try it!
>
> Save this for when you start learning scales.

### 6. `tip-staff-mnemonics.jpg`
> Still counting lines to find a note? These 4 sayings fix that. 📝
>
> Treble clef lines (bottom to top): E G B D F, "Every Good Boy Does Fine"
> Treble clef spaces: F A C E (they spell FACE)
>
> Bass clef lines: G B D F A, "Good Boys Do Fine Always"
> Bass clef spaces: A C E G, "All Cows Eat Grass"
>
> What saying did your teacher use? There are lots of versions!

### 7. `tip-note-values.jpg`
> How long does each note last? (in 4/4 time) ⏱️
>
> Whole note = 4 beats
> Half note = 2 beats
> Quarter note = 1 beat
> Eighth note = ½ beat
>
> A dot after a note adds half its value, so a dotted half note lasts 3 beats.
>
> Counting out loud ("1 and 2 and…") feels silly for a week, then it changes everything.

### 8. `tip-15-minute-practice-routine.jpg`
> No time to practise? You have 15 minutes. ⏰
>
> 2 min: warm up (slow scales or five-finger patterns)
> 5 min: the hard part, only the bars you can't play yet, slowly
> 5 min: review something you already know
> 3 min: play for fun
>
> Try it every day this week and tell me on Sunday how it went. 💪

### 9. `tip-piano-posture-checklist.jpg`
> Tension in your shoulders or wrists after playing? Check this before your next session. 🪑
>
> ✔️ Bench height: forearms level with the keys
> ✔️ Sit on the front half of the bench
> ✔️ Feet flat on the floor (a footstool for kids)
> ✔️ Curved fingers, as if holding a ball; wrists level
> ✔️ Shoulders down. If they creep up, stop and shake out
>
> Good posture isn't about looking right. It's what lets you play longer without pain.

### 10. `tip-sharps-and-flats.jpg`
> Why does every black key have two names? 🤔
>
> ♯ Sharp = one key to the right (a half step higher)
> ♭ Flat = one key to the left (a half step lower)
>
> So the black key between C and D is C♯ when you think "up from C" and D♭ when you think "down from D". Same key, same sound, two names.
>
> Did this ever confuse you? You're not alone!

## Text posts that start conversations (no image needed)

Post these between the cheat sheets. Add a photo of your own piano or sheet music if you have one: real photos do better than stock images.

1. > What was the very first song you learned to play on the piano? 🎹 Mine is in the comments.
2. > Be honest: how many minutes did you practise this week? No judgement here. ⏱️
3. > Finish the sentence: "I wish I'd known ______ when I started piano."
4. > Adult learners: what made you start (or restart) the piano? 🎶
5. > What's the one piece you dream of being able to play one day?
6. > Digital piano or acoustic: which are you playing on right now, and would you switch?
7. > Sight-reading, rhythm, or hand independence: which is hardest for you right now?
8. > Teachers and parents: what's the best trick you've found for getting kids to practise?
9. > A little reminder: playing slowly *and correctly* is faster progress than playing fast and fixing mistakes later. 🐢 What piece are you slowing down this week?
10. > If you could ask a piano teacher one question, what would it be? Ask below and I'll answer as many as I can. 👇
11. > Hands up if you've ever practised a piece so much you started hearing it in your sleep. 🙋 Which piece?
12. > Fun fact: the piano is a percussion and a string instrument at the same time. Its strings make the sound, but they're *struck* by hammers. What's your favourite piano fact?
