How the chord-first method works

Most methods teach you to read a melody note by note, then add the left hand, then chords, over months. Pianoforall runs the other way:

  1. Chord shapes first. You learn the main major and minor chords as hand shapes, starting with C, F and G.
  2. Rhythm patterns. You play those chords in 15 popular rhythm styles (rock, ballad, waltz, boogie and more), so three chords already sound like music.
  3. Styles. Blues, ballads and jazz build on the same chords, with improvising and playing by ear.
  4. Reading and technique later. Sheet music, classical pieces, scales and exercises in every key come once you're already playing.

What I liked, and what I didn't

What I liked

  • It keeps adults going. Sounding good early is the biggest reason adults keep practising, and nothing on the market does this better.
  • Real musicianship. Playing by ear, improvising and making up your own arrangements are skills most methods leave for years, or forever.

What I didn't

  • No feedback. Nothing hears you play. Record yourself every week or two, or you won't notice uneven rhythm.
  • Theory without the why. You'll know the shapes before you understand them. It's fine for most players, but it's a gap if you want to analyse music.
  • Weak on sight-reading. Reading comes late and stays light.
  • Dated production. The videos and books look older than the apps. They work, but they won't wow you.

How to get the most out of your 7-day free trial

  1. Day 1: start the trial and open Book 1 (Rhythm Style). Learn C, F and G as shapes, then the first rhythm.
  2. Days 2–4: 20 minutes a day. One new rhythm a day, played over the same three chords. Don't rush ahead.
  3. Day 5: record yourself playing two rhythms back to back. Listen for an even beat.
  4. Day 6: try the first ballad tricks and a simple song progression.
  5. Day 7: decide. If you're enjoying it and practising most days, keep going. If not, cancel before the trial ends. Even after you pay, the 60-day guarantee covers you.

What you'll need: any piano or keyboard. I recommend an 88-key digital piano with weighted keys, such as the Yamaha P-145. Pianoforall needs no cable or app connection.
