What is Piano Marvel?

Piano Marvel is a piano lesson and practice app that listens to you play and scores you on note and rhythm accuracy. It was created by its founder and CEO, Aaron Garner, and is built around four sections:

  • Method: a step-by-step course from first notes to classical repertoire, with short video lessons and graded exercises.
  • Library: 25,000+ songs and pieces (the App Store listing says 30,000+), classical and popular, including sheet music licensed from Hal Leonard and Alfred Music. You can also upload your own music.
  • Sight Reading: the SASR test and hundreds of reading drills. This is the section that sets it apart.
  • Technique: scales, chords, arpeggios and exercises, scored the same way.

It runs on iPhone, iPad, Android, Windows, Mac and Chromebook. On iPhone and iPad it needs iOS 15 or later, and the App Store rating is 4.7 from about 5,500 ratings (October 2026).

How a Piano Marvel lesson works

Every exercise, song and test follows the same loop. It's the reason the app works.

  1. Watch the short video if there is one (Method lessons have them).
  2. Practise the piece: slow the tempo down, play hands separately and loop a hard bar. The app follows you as you play.
  3. Assess: play it through at tempo. The app scores note and rhythm accuracy from 0 to 100% and shows where you slipped.
  4. Repeat until you earn the score to move on. Trophies, streaks, challenges and leaderboards are there if they motivate you, and easy to ignore if they don't.

The habit it builds is the one teachers try to build by hand: slow, accurate practice, then a real run-through, then an honest result. It's also what App Store reviewers praise most: a scored run-through catches rhythm slips you don't hear yourself.

The SASR sight-reading test, explained

SASR stands for Standard Assessment of Sight Reading. It's a test that gives your sight-reading a score, so you know your level and can watch it improve. Nothing else on the app market does this as well. Here's exactly how it runs:

  1. You pick a starting point: beginner, intermediate or advanced.
  2. A short piece you've never seen appears. You get 20 seconds to look it over, then a countdown, and you play.
  3. Score 80% or more and the next piece is harder. Score under 80% and you get a strike.
  4. After three strikes the test ends and you get your SASR score.

The pieces are spread across 18 levels split into 90 sublevels, from 1a to 18e. Each piece was placed by averaging the scores of a team of about 40 sight-readers, so a level means the same thing for everyone. The top score is 1,900. Piano Marvel's own examples give a sense of scale: a score of 189 or below is an early beginner, and 1,436 is the level of a professional accompanist.

How to use it: take the test in week one, practise the reading drills for 10 minutes a day, and retest every two to four weeks. A rising score shows progress that's otherwise hard to feel, which is why so many adult learners say it keeps them practising.

Not sure where you'd land? Piano Marvel has a free sample test on its site, and the full test is free with an account.

The Method course

The Method is the guided path, and it reads like a well-planned method book: notes and rhythm first, then hands together, scales and chords, and later classical pieces from the main periods. Each step adds one idea, with a short video and a set of exercises you must pass to move on. Theory comes in as you need it, not in separate lectures.

It suits anyone who likes structure: adults who want to "learn properly", children with a parent or teacher keeping them on track, and returning players filling gaps. It's less suited to someone who wants to play a pop song this weekend. The early levels are exercises, not hits.

The song library

Premium opens 25,000+ songs and pieces at every level, from easy classics to film and pop arrangements, including sheet music licensed from publishers such as Hal Leonard and Alfred Music. Every piece is scored the same way as a lesson, which turns your repertoire into practice.

You can also upload your own music (MusicXML or MIDI files) and slice it into practice sections. It's powerful but fiddly. Several App Store reviewers say uploads are hard to manage on iPad, so do this part on a computer.

What you need: MIDI or microphone

Piano Marvel listens to you in one of two ways, and the choice matters more than in most apps.

  • MIDI (recommended). Connect a digital piano or keyboard to your tablet, phone or computer. The app then knows exactly which key you pressed and when, so the scoring is precise. Most current digital pianos have a "USB to Host" port: you need a USB-B to USB-C (or Lightning, or USB-A) cable, about $10. Some pianos also connect by Bluetooth MIDI, with no cable at all.
  • Microphone. For acoustic pianos. Piano Marvel calls this mode new, and results vary with room noise and the piano. It's fine for trying the app, but for scored tests a MIDI connection is far more reliable.

On which piano: use an 88-key digital piano with weighted keys. The method's later levels use the full range of the keyboard, and weighted keys build the finger strength you'll need on any real piano. The Yamaha P-145, our top beginner pick, connects with one USB cable. Teachers who have reviewed Piano Marvel note that setup can be finicky the first time: you need a MIDI-capable keyboard, the right cable and a steady internet connection. Once it's connected, choose MIDI as the input in the app's settings and play a few notes to check it hears you.

What's good, and what could be better

What's good

  • It teaches reading. Real notation from day one, and a test that proves you're improving.
  • Feedback is honest. A score you can't fudge. Adults practise better with a number to beat.
  • It works for every level. Beginners get the Method. Intermediate and advanced players get SASR, Technique and the library.
  • Good value. The cheapest subscription app on our best online piano lessons list, with a free plan you can learn on.

What could be better

  • It feels like homework. The look is busy and serious. If you need fun to keep going, a game-style app may suit you better.
  • Scoring isn't perfect. App Store reviewers mention grace notes scored as full notes and the odd wrong fingering. It's rare, but it's annoying in a test.
  • Support can be slow, according to several reviews. The developer does reply to reviews on the App Store.
  • Little for ear players. There's ear training, but improvising, chord-based playing and playing by ear aren't its focus.

What other users say

Across Reddit's piano-learning communities, Piano Marvel is one of the most recommended apps for sight-reading. Readers praise the reading drills, the theory and the structure, and often describe it as the closest an app comes to traditional lessons. The common complaints: it's less fun than Simply Piano or Flowkey, and it works best with a MIDI cable. On the App Store it holds 4.7 out of 5 from about 5,500 ratings. Praise goes to the structure and the instant feedback, and the low ratings mostly mention scoring glitches, slow support and uploads.

Your first week with Piano Marvel

  1. Day 1: create a free account, connect your digital piano with a USB cable, and pick MIDI as the input. Play a few notes to check that it hears you.

Piano Marvel FAQ

Is Piano Marvel free?

Yes, there's a free plan with 150+ songs, 200+ lesson exercises, 25 video lessons and scored feedback. Premium, with 25,000+ songs, 1,200+ exercises and unlimited SASR tests, costs $17.99 a month or $129.99 a year.

Is Piano Marvel good for beginners?

Yes, especially adult beginners who want to learn to read music. The Method course starts from your first notes. Beginners who mainly want to play popular songs quickly may prefer Pianoforall or Flowkey.

Does Piano Marvel work with an acoustic piano?

Yes, through its microphone mode, but results vary with the room and the piano. For accurate scoring, use a digital piano or keyboard connected by USB or Bluetooth MIDI.

What devices does Piano Marvel work on?

iPhone and iPad (iOS 15 or later), Android, Windows, Mac and Chromebook.

What is a good SASR score?

Scores go up to 1,900. Piano Marvel describes 189 or below as an early beginner and about 1,436 as the level of a professional accompanist. What matters most is your own score rising over time. Retest every two to four weeks.

Is Piano Marvel better than Simply Piano?

For learning to read music, yes. Simply Piano is more playful and better for young children who need games to stay engaged.

How do I cancel Piano Marvel?

Cancel where you subscribed. In the App Store or Google Play, turn off auto-renew in your subscriptions at least 24 hours before the period ends. If you subscribed on the website, cancel from your Piano Marvel account or ask its support team. Your account then returns to the free plan.
