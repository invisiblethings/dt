Your **page** already has more referring domains than the #1 and #2 pages. What you lack is **site-wide authority** (5 times fewer referring domains than Pianodreamers) and **topical depth**.

## What the winners do that you don't

1. **A full review behind every pick.** Pianodreamers links each model to its own in-depth review: FP-30X, PX-S1100, P-225, ES120, P-145, P-125, DGX-670, FP-10, FP-60X, ES920, Liano and more. Pianist's Compass does the same for 14 models. Google sees a site that has examined each instrument. You have one piano review (P-145) behind 11 picks. **This is the biggest gap.**
2. **Original media, a lot of it.**
   - Pianodreamers has 100+ of its own photos per page (main, back and control shots of each model) and 9–10 of its own YouTube demos.
   - You have 20 images, and no video.
   - Photos and sound demos are the strongest proof of hands-on testing, which is what Google's reviews guidance asks for.
3. **Trust up front.** Pianist's Compass opens with "Why you should trust me" and "How I chose and tested". Pianodreamers opens with "How Did We Choose?". Both name a person with schema (Person). Yours has "How I Tested" near the end, and the author details conflict, as noted in the earlier audit.
4. **Headings that carry the query.** Pianodreamers' H2s read "Best Intermediate Digital Piano Under $1000 – Roland FP-30X" and "Best Advanced Digital Piano Under $2,000 – Kawai ES920". Each heading matches a long-tail search: level, price and model. Yours are "1. Roland FP-30X — Best for Long-Term Growth".
5. **Stage pianos in the mix.** Pianist's Compass's #2 page is "for home *and stage*": Roland RD-2000 EX, Yamaha CP88, Nord Stage 4, Studiologic Numa. That covers the advanced and stage searches your page doesn't.
6. **Specs in a scannable form.** MusicRadar has 10 spec tables and ItemList schema. Pianodreamers uses product boxes with key specs and prices. You have none.
7. **Age and steady updates.** Pianodreamers' pages date from 2017 and 2019 and are refreshed regularly. That history helps, and you can only match it by keeping your page updated over time.

## What it tells us about the results page

- **#3 is a Reddit thread and #5 is a 61-word tag page.** Google is filling places with weak results, so the top 5 is beatable for a page that clearly shows real testing.
- The two strongest competitors win on **depth across the site** (many model reviews) and **original media**, not on the length of one page. Pianist's Compass ranks #2 with 4,303 words, less than your 5,234.

## What to change, in priority order

1. **Write model reviews for the picks** (the topical map already lists some). Start with:
   - Roland FP-30X, Roland FP-10, Casio PX-S1100
   - then Kawai ES920, Yamaha P-225, Roland FP-90X, Kawai KDP120, Yamaha CLP-835

   Link each pick in the hub with "Read my full Roland FP-30X review". Each review also targets "[model] review" searches.
2. **Add your own media.**
   - At least one real photo per pick (keys, controls, rear panel).
   - Short sound or action clips. Even a 30-second phone video per model, uploaded to YouTube and embedded, puts you ahead of most competitors.
3. **Move trust to the top**: a short "Why trust me / how I tested" box under the quick answer, with the author's real credentials and Person schema. Fix the author details as the audit described.
4. **Rewrite the pick headings to match searches**, for example "Best Intermediate Digital Piano Under $1,000 – Roland FP-30X".
5. **Add the comparison table and verdict cards** (ItemList) from GSC-AUDIT.md.
6. **Add a stage-piano section** (RD-88 / RD-2000 EX, CP73/CP88, Nord Piano 5 / Stage 4, Numa X). The hub already ranks #4 for "best stage piano" without one.
7. **Build site-wide authority:**
   - Pitch your guides to piano teachers' blogs, music schools and forums where they truly help.
   - Get listed on teacher directories (you're already on onlinepianoteachers.com).
   - Create one or two linkable assets, such as a "Digital piano action comparison chart" or "Every 88-key digital piano by weight and price".

Effort and expected payoff: items 3–6 can be done in the next rewrite of the hub. Items 1, 2 and 7 build over months, and they're what keeps the #1 and #2 sites ahead today.
