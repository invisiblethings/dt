Best digital pianos with weighted keys: what the actions mean

Every piano on this page has fully weighted, graded hammer-action keys: heavier in the bass and lighter in the treble, like an acoustic piano. Brands give their actions different names, and the name tells you more about how a piano will feel than any other spec.

Three terms are worth knowing. Escapement (Kawai calls it let-off) is the small notch you feel when you press a key slowly, copied from a grand piano. Triple-sensor keys detect fast repeated notes more reliably. Counterweights and wooden keys make the keys feel more even along their length, which matters most when you play near the fallboard. As a teacher, my advice is simple: try to play an entry-level and a step-up action side by side before you decide whether the difference is worth paying for.

Electric piano vs digital piano vs keyboard: what's the difference?

In everyday speech, “electric piano” and “digital piano” mean the same thing: an 88-key instrument with weighted keys that sounds like a piano and never needs tuning. Strictly speaking, an electric piano is a vintage instrument like the Rhodes or Wurlitzer, whose sound comes from struck tines or reeds. A keyboard is different again: usually 61 lighter, unweighted keys, hundreds of sounds and a lower price. It's great for fun and songwriting, but not enough for learning classical technique.

How to choose a digital piano: what actually matters

In order of importance: the key action, the piano sound, the speakers, then everything else.

  • Key action: 88 fully weighted, graded hammer-action keys. This is what builds finger strength and control, and it's the hardest thing to upgrade later.
  • Piano sound: sampled (Yamaha, Kawai, most Rolands) or modeled (Roland's top models). Both can sound wonderful; listen through headphones and speakers.
  • Polyphony: 64 notes is enough for beginner pieces; 128 or more helps with heavy pedalling and layered sounds, and 192 to 256 is plenty.
  • Speakers: portable speakers are fine in a small room. A console's cabinet speakers sound much fuller.
  • Pedals: a sustain pedal is essential. For classical music, three pedals with half-pedal support are worth having. Consoles include them; portables usually need an add-on unit.
  • Connectivity: USB or Bluetooth MIDI for learning apps, and Bluetooth audio if you want to play along with recordings.

A digital piano also never needs tuning, unlike an acoustic one (see how to tune a piano). Once you have one, my guides to the best online piano lessons and how to teach yourself piano will help you make the most of it.

Where to buy and try a digital piano

Play before you buy if you possibly can, because key action is personal. Guitar Center stocks the popular Yamaha, Roland and Casio models; local piano dealers are often the best place to try Kawai and Clavinova consoles; and online retailers such as Sweetwater, Kraft Music and Amazon are convenient for portables. If you buy online, choose a seller with a return window of at least 30 days, and buy from an authorized dealer so the warranty is valid.

Digital piano FAQ

What is the best digital piano for most people?

The Roland FP-30X. At $649.99 it combines a very good hammer action, 256-note polyphony, 11 W + 11 W speakers and Bluetooth audio and MIDI. That's enough piano for years of progress.

What is the best digital piano for beginners?

The Yamaha P-145BT or the Roland FP-10, both about $500. If piano sound matters most, the Kawai ES60 is excellent at a similar price. My best beginner pianos guide compares seven options.

What is the best digital piano under $1,000?

The Roland FP-30X for most people. Choose the Yamaha P-225 ($769.99) if you prefer Yamaha's CFX sound, or the Kawai ES120 ($899 to $999) for Kawai's tone and Bluetooth audio.

What is the best digital piano for advanced players?

The Kawai ES920 is the best portable, with Responsive Hammer III keys and 20 W + 20 W speakers. For the home, the Yamaha CLP-835 or Kawai CN301 consoles are the next step up.

Is an electric piano the same as a digital piano?

In everyday use, yes: both mean an 88-key instrument with weighted keys and piano sounds. Strictly, an electric piano is a vintage Rhodes or Wurlitzer type of instrument.

Do I need weighted keys?

Yes, if you want to learn piano. Graded hammer-action keys build the finger strength and control you need, and make it easy to move to an acoustic piano later.

How much should I spend on a digital piano?

About $500 gets you a good beginner instrument, $650 to $1,000 a piano you won't outgrow for years, and $1,700 and up an action and speakers for advanced playing.

Is a console digital piano better than a portable?

For a piano that stays at home, usually yes: cabinet speakers sound fuller and three pedals are included. A portable is cheaper for the same action and easier to move.

Do digital pianos need tuning?

No. Their sound is sampled or modeled, so they never go out of tune.

How many keys should a digital piano have?

88, like an acoustic piano. Every model on this page has 88 weighted keys.
