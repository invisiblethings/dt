Yamaha P-145 / P-145BT Review in 2026: Still My #1 Pick for Beginners

Richard J. Abraham Pianist since age 8, gigging player and teacher of hundreds of kids and adults. I started Pianoers to give straight, tested advice on piano gear and lessons. Updated Oct 7, 2026 7 min read
On this page

    If you're starting piano right now and want something that actually feels good, sounds legit, and won't make you quit in three months… the Yamaha P-145 (or its Bluetooth sibling, the P-145BT) is still sitting at the absolute top of my beginner recommendations in 2026.

    I’ve had the original P-145 since it launched in 2023, hammered on it almost daily, lent it to students, moved it around apartments like it’s a laptop… and then in 2025 Yamaha released the P-145BT with the one upgrade everyone begged for. So yeah, I bought that one too. Spoiler: everything I’m about to say applies to both unless I call it out.

    P-145 vs P-145BT: What’s the Difference?

    Short answer: almost nothing except the feature that matters most to 90% of beginners.

    ModelBluetooth audioEverything elsePrice (Oct 2026)
    P-145No (USB cable only)IdenticalBeing phased out; Yamaha USA now lists only the BT
    P-145BTYes: stream music from your phoneIdentical$499.99 list, often on sale

    That’s literally it. Same keys, same sound, same speakers, same weight. The BT version just lets you stream Spotify, YouTube or lesson audio straight through the piano’s speakers. It’s Bluetooth audio: the Smart Pianist app still connects with a USB cable (or Yamaha’s UD-BT01 wireless MIDI adaptor, sold separately). If you’re buying new today → just get the P-145BT and thank me later.

    How the P-145 Feels and Sounds

    Sit down and play one chord. Done. You’re sold.

    The Graded Hammer Compact (GHC) action is quieter than the old P-125/P-45, lighter than Roland/Casio competitors, but still properly graded (heavy in the bass, lighter up top). Fast repeats? Smooth and silent. No cheap clacking. I can practice at 1 a.m. without waking anyone, and it still teaches correct finger technique. Students who switch to an acoustic upright later barely notice the difference.

    Sound comes from Yamaha’s CFIIIS concert grand sample; bright, punchy, super clean. Not the absolute richest tone on the planet (Roland wins there), but easily the most “motivating” for beginners. Through headphones it’s stunning. Through the built-in 2×7W speakers it’s loud and clear enough for a bedroom or small apartment, way better than the downward-firing Rolands that get muffled on a desk.

    Yamaha P-145 Specs: What You Get

    • 88 fully weighted GHC keys (quieter, smoother, lighter than most at this price)
    • 64-note polyphony (plenty until you’re playing advanced Liszt)
    • CFIIIS grand piano sound + 9 other usable voices
    • Damper resonance, 4 reverb types, Duo mode (splits the keyboard into two identical halves for lessons)
    • USB audio + MIDI interface (record straight into GarageBand or FL Studio with one cable)
    • Smart Pianist and Rec’n’Share apps (over USB)
    • Optional three-pedal unit (LP-5A)
    • Weighs 24.5 lbs (11.1 kg): stupidly portable
    • Runs on the included AC adaptor (no battery option)
    • P-145BT only: Bluetooth audio (stream and play along wirelessly)

    Who the P-145 Is Best For

    • Total beginners who want forgiving, quiet keys
    • Adults returning to piano (no childish lights or 600 sounds)
    • Tiny apartments / dorms / frequent movers
    • Anyone who practices mostly with headphones or wants to jam over YouTube beats (P-145BT shines here)

    Downsides

    • Speakers are good, not amazing. Fine unplugged, but you’ll eventually want headphones or external speakers for bigger rooms.
    • 64-note polyphony is on the low side. Beginners never hit the limit, but heavy sustain-pedal classical players might, years down the line.
    • No battery power, so it isn’t a busk-in-the-park keyboard.
    • The original P-145 needs a cable for backing tracks. The P-145BT fixes that, but the app still wants a USB cable on both.

    Yamaha P-145 vs P-45

    I owned and taught on the P-45 for years. Yamaha replaced it with the P-145 in 2023, so today you’ll mostly find it used or as leftover stock. Here’s the real difference:

    Quick Comparison

    Key Action
    P-145 / BT: GHC – quieter, smoother, lighter
    P-45: GHS – classic but clackier
    Winner: P-145
    Key Noise

    Winner: P-145
    Sound

    P-45: Older sample – still great but flat
    Winner: P-145
    Speakers

    P-45: 2×6W
    Winner: P-145
    Connectivity
    P-145 / BT: USB audio + MIDI, Smart Pianist (BT adds Bluetooth audio)
    P-45: USB MIDI only
    Winner: P-145BT
    Size
    P-145 / BT: 10.6" deep, 24.5 lbs
    P-45: 11.5" deep, 25.3 lbs
    Winner: P-145
    Price
    P-145BT: $499.99 list, often on sale
    P-45: Used or leftover stock only
    Winner: P-45 (if you find a deal)

    Bottom Line

    • If you find a P-45 in great shape under $420 → buy it. Still fantastic.
    • If the gap to the P-145BT is $50 or less → get the P-145BT. Quieter keys + Bluetooth audio are 100% worth it in 2026.

    I sold my P-45 the week the P-145 arrived. Enough said.

    My Verdict After Three Years (and Owning Both Versions)

    I’ve recommended the P-series to dozens of friends and students. Zero regrets. Zero returns. People text me months later saying they’re addicted.

    The P-145 was already the safe, no-drama king of beginner digital pianos. The P-145BT just removed the last tiny annoyance and made it basically perfect under $600.

    If you find a screaming deal on a leftover non-BT P-145 → still buy it. Otherwise? Get the P-145BT. You’ll use that Bluetooth every single day.

    Pair it with a good stand, bench, some closed-back headphones, and you’re set for the next 5–10 years easy. Way better than wasting money on a flashy 61-key toy you’ll outgrow in six months.

    Yamaha P-145 FAQ

    Is the Yamaha P-145 / P-145BT good for complete beginners?

    Yes. Hands down one of the best pianos for beginners you can buy right now. The keys are quiet, forgiving, and teach proper technique without fighting you. I’ve put total newbies on it and they’re still practicing months later instead of quitting.

    What’s the difference between the P-145 and P-145BT?

    Only Bluetooth audio. Everything else (keys, sound, speakers, weight) is identical. The BT version lets you stream backing tracks or YouTube lessons wirelessly through the piano’s speakers. The Smart Pianist app connects by USB cable on both. If you’re buying new, just get the P-145BT.

    Is the Yamaha P-145BT the best digital piano under $600 in 2026?

    For most beginners and budget-conscious adults, yes, still my #1. The Roland FP-10 is the closest rival, but the lighter, quieter Yamaha action + Bluetooth audio edges it out unless you specifically want a heavier “acoustic” feel. More options in my best digital pianos guide.

    Is the Yamaha P-145 still worth buying or is it discontinued?

    The original P-145 is being phased out: Yamaha USA now lists only the P-145BT, and new non-BT stock is drying up. If you find a leftover one at a good price, it’s the same piano minus Bluetooth. Otherwise grab the P-145BT. Zero regrets.

    Does the P-145 / P-145BT have weighted keys?

    Yes, 88 fully weighted, graded hammer keys (heavier in the bass, lighter in the treble) with the newer GHC mechanism. Feels way closer to a real piano than any “semi-weighted” or springy keyboard nonsense.
