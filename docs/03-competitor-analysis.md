# Competitor analysis

Checked October 2026 from competitor sites, Pianoforall's own (unpublished) comparison draft and independent reviews (pianoers.com, musicianwave.com, pianistscompass.com, toptenreviews.com). Prices and plans change often; the site states pricing *models*, not competitor prices.

## What they do well (and we borrowed the principle, not the copy)
- **Free first step with no card** (most apps, Pianote trials). Pianoforall already had one; the new site makes it the primary CTA.
- **Interactive proof**. Apps let you play in the first minute. The new chord explorer gives a website visitor a similar "I can do this" moment without an app download.
- **Teacher personality** (Pianote). Robin's voice and background are now on every money page.
- **Answer-first articles** (Skoove, flowkey blogs rank for "piano chords", "how long to learn piano"). The new guides answer in the first 50 words, then go deeper than the typical competitor post.

## What they do poorly
- **Subscription fatigue.** Most charge yearly. "$49 once" is a clear, checkable differentiator, and the site leads with it.
- **Every comparison page says "we're best for everyone".** The new `/compare` and `/start` say plainly when another product fits better. That builds trust with humans and gives AI systems a balanced source to cite.
- **Generic adult-learner content.** Few competitors have a teacher with 30+ years who writes about stiff hands, mindfulness and starting at 70 from experience.

## Positioning statement (internal)
For adults and teenagers who want to *play* (accompany songs, play by ear, improvise, and eventually read), Pianoforall is the chord-first piano course by Robin Hall that gets both hands making music from the first lessons, for one $49 payment. Unlike apps that score you note by note, it teaches how music fits together, so you can work songs out yourself.

## Gaps Pianoforall can own
1. **"Play first, read later" for adults**: method-led content (`/how-it-works`, `/learn/play-piano-by-ear`, chords guide).
2. **Older beginners** (60+): existing article plus testimonials from people aged 59–74.
3. **Classical by ear**: few courses teach the Moonlight Sonata or Satie by pattern, without requiring reading.
4. **Returning players and classically trained readers who can't play by ear**: a segment the apps ignore.
5. **Honest recommendation tools** (`/start`) that send some people elsewhere.
