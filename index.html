Tom Brier, the modern master of ragtime

Tom Brier (born October 3, 1971) is an American ragtime pianist and composer. He has written more than 200 piano pieces, among them the modern ragtime standard “Razor Blades,” and spent three decades playing ragtime festivals across the United States, known for his powerful playing and his knack for sight-reading almost anything put in front of him.

“Razor Blades,” Tom Brier’s best-known rag

Written in 1994, “Razor Blades” has become a standard of the modern ragtime repertoire. It has been described as a modern counterpart to Scott Joplin’s “Maple Leaf Rag.” Press play to hear it.

Recognition

In 2023 the Scott Joplin International Ragtime Foundation presented Tom Brier with its Ragtime Outstanding Achievement Award, accepted on his behalf by his longtime friend Warren Jennings at the Scott Joplin Ragtime Festival in Sedalia, Missouri. His recordings with Nan Bostick of “Borneo Rag” and “Indian Summer” were also featured in Ken Burns’s 2005 documentary Unforgivable Blackness: The Rise and Fall of Jack Johnson, and the annual Tom Brier Scholarship at the Historic Sutter Creek Ragtime Festival carries his name.
