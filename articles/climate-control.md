# Piano Humidity and Temperature: The Ideal Range and How to Hold It

Keep your piano at 40 to 50 percent relative humidity and a steady room temperature around 68 to 72°F (20 to 22°C). Steinway's own guidance allows a wider band, 40 to 60 percent and 68 to 78°F, and the in-piano climate systems technicians install hold 42 percent. Every source agrees on the part that matters more than the number: keep the humidity steady. A piano that sits at 45 percent all year will outlast and out-tune one that swings from 30 percent in January to 65 percent in July.

This guide covers what humidity and temperature do to a piano, how to measure them, and what to buy to keep them where they belong.

## Humidity at a glance

| Relative humidity | What happens to the piano |
|---|---|
| Below 30% | Risk of soundboard cracks, loose tuning pins, failed glue joints |
| 30 to 40% | Pitch drifts flat, keys may click or feel loose |
| 40 to 50% | Ideal. Tuning and action stay stable |
| 50 to 60% | Pitch drifts sharp, action starts to feel heavy |
| Above 60% | Sticking keys, rusting strings and pins, risk of mold |

## Why humidity matters so much

Most of a piano is wood: the soundboard, the bridges, the pinblock that holds the tuning pins, the keys, and thousands of small action parts. Wood takes on moisture from humid air and gives it back to dry air, swelling and shrinking as it goes. Felt and cloth in the action do the same.

A few percent of movement in a thin spruce soundboard or a felt bushing is enough to change how the piano sounds and plays. Repeated year after year, the same movement opens cracks and loosens joints.

## What high humidity does

Above about 50 percent, the wood and felt swell.

- **Keys stick or are slow to come back up.** Swollen key bushings grip the guide pins, and the whole action feels heavy.
- **The pitch goes sharp.** The soundboard swells, pushes up on the bridges, and raises the tension on every string.
- **Strings and tuning pins rust.** Rusty strings sound dull and are more likely to break when a technician tunes them.
- **Mold can grow** on felts and inside the case if the room stays damp for weeks.

## What low humidity does

Below about 40 percent, the wood dries and shrinks.

- **The pitch goes flat.** The soundboard loses its upward curve, called crown, and string tension drops.
- **Tuning pins loosen.** The pinblock shrinks around the pins, so the piano drifts out of tune faster and some pins stop holding at all.
- **The soundboard can crack.** Cracks often show up after a long, dry heating season.
- **Keys click or wobble**, and glue joints in the action and case can let go.

## Why your piano goes out of tune every season

Seasonal tuning drift comes from humidity. Humid summer air swells the soundboard and the piano goes sharp. Once the heating comes on in winter and the air dries, the soundboard shrinks and the piano goes flat. Treble and bass don't move by the same amount, so the piano drifts out of tune with itself as well as off pitch.

Temperature on its own moves the pitch far less. The reason a piano sounds worse in a cold, dry winter is the dry air, not the cold.

This is also why the timing of a tuning matters. A piano tuned at the dry end of winter will be sharp by summer. Tuning once the season has settled, and holding the humidity steady, makes each tuning last.

## Temperature: steady beats exact

Aim for a room that stays around 68 to 72°F, which is a comfortable room for people too. The danger is fast change rather than any particular temperature.

- **Direct sunlight** heats one side of the case, fades the finish, and dries the wood under it.
- **Radiators, heating vents, air conditioning vents, and fireplaces** blast hot or cold, dry air across the piano. Keep it well clear of all of them.
- **Exterior walls and windows** run colder in winter and warmer in summer than the room around them.
- **Cold rooms** are less of a problem than the moment they warm up. Warm, damp air hitting cold strings and plate can leave condensation, and condensation becomes rust. Warm an unheated room over several hours, not all at once.

After a move, give the piano time to settle in its new room. Most technicians suggest waiting a couple of weeks before tuning, because the soundboard needs that long to adjust to the new humidity.

## How to measure the humidity around your piano

You can't manage what you don't measure. A digital hygrometer costs less than a single tuning and tells you which problem you have.

- **Put it near the piano**, at about the height of the keys and away from vents and windows, so it reads the air the piano sits in.
- **Check it weekly**, and more often when the heating or air conditioning first comes on.
- **Calibrate it once.** Cheap hygrometers can read several points high or low. Seal it in a bag with a small dish of damp salt (salt with a few drops of water, not dissolved) for eight hours or so. The air in the bag settles at about 75 percent; the difference from your reading is the error to allow for.

## How to control humidity

Most homes need help in one direction for part of the year and the other direction for the rest. There are two ways to give it.

### Control the room

- **A humidifier for dry months.** Choose a model with a built-in humidistat so it stops when the room reaches your target. Choose an evaporative humidifier where you can. Ultrasonic humidifiers filled with tap water throw out a fine white mineral dust that settles inside the piano; if you use one, fill it with distilled water.
- **A dehumidifier for damp months**, also with a humidistat, so it doesn't overdry the room.
- **Air conditioning** removes moisture as it cools, which helps in humid summers.
- **Never point a humidifier, dehumidifier, or vent at the piano.** Place the unit across the room and let the whole room reach the target.

## Where to put your piano

1. Against an interior wall, not an exterior one
2. Out of direct sunlight at every time of day
3. Well away from radiators, vents, and fireplaces
4. Away from doors and windows that open to the outside
5. Not in a basement, garage, conservatory, or bathroom-adjacent room, where humidity swings hardest

A breathable cover keeps off dust and sun, but it does nothing for humidity, so don't rely on one.

## A seasonal routine

- **Start of the heating season:** check the hygrometer and start the humidifier as soon as the reading drops below 40 percent.
- **Midwinter:** check weekly. This is when cracks and loose pins start.
- **Spring:** turn the humidifier off as the air softens.
- **Summer:** run the dehumidifier or air conditioning if the reading climbs above 50 percent.
- **Tuning:** Steinway recommends two to four tunings a year for a piano played about an hour a day. Book them after each season has settled rather than at the start of a swing. Our guide to [when your piano needs tuning](https://pianoers.com/piano-tuning-when-and-why-its-needed/) covers the signs.

## Signs your piano has a humidity problem

| What you notice | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Keys stick or are slow to come back up | Humidity too high |
| The piano sounds sharp, or tunings don't last through summer | Humidity too high |
| Rust on the strings or tuning pins | Humidity too high, or condensation |
| The piano sounds flat after winter | Humidity too low |
| Tuning pins turn with little resistance, or the piano won't hold a tuning | Humidity too low, often for several winters |
| Keys click or feel loose | Humidity too low |
| A buzz that wasn't there before | Humidity too low: a soundboard crack or loose rib |

If you see the dry-air signs, raise the humidity a few points at a time rather than all at once, and book a technician to check the soundboard and pinblock.

## Frequently asked questions

### What humidity should a piano be kept at?

Between 40 and 50 percent relative humidity, with 45 percent a good target. Steinway allows 40 to 60 percent, and in-piano climate systems hold 42 percent. Keeping the level steady matters more than hitting an exact number.

### What temperature is best for a piano?

A steady room temperature of about 68 to 72°F (20 to 22°C). Steinway's guidance allows 68 to 78°F. Avoid fast changes, direct sunlight, and heating or cooling vents.

### Does cold weather damage a piano?

Cold by itself does little harm. The risks come from the dry air that winter heating creates, and from warming a cold piano too fast, which can leave condensation on the strings. Warm a cold room over several hours.

### Why does my piano go out of tune every season?

Because the humidity changes. Humid summer air swells the soundboard and pushes the pitch sharp; dry winter air shrinks it and pulls the pitch flat. Steady humidity makes tunings last longer.

### Do I need a humidifier for my piano?

If your hygrometer reads below 40 percent for weeks at a time, which in most homes means the heating season, yes. Use a room humidifier with a humidistat, evaporative if possible, placed away from the piano.

### Can high humidity cause piano keys to stick?

Yes. Above about 50 percent, the wooden keys and the felt bushings around their guide pins swell, so keys stick or are slow to come back up. Bringing the humidity down frees most of them within days.

### Is a Dampp-Chaser system worth it?

It's worth it in rooms you can't control, such as old houses with big seasonal swings, churches, and schools. It holds the inside of the piano at about 42 percent and costs about $650 to $1,000 installed. In a well-controlled room, a hygrometer and a room humidifier do the same job for less.

### How long should a piano settle after moving before tuning?

Most technicians suggest a couple of weeks, so the soundboard can adjust to the humidity in its new room. A tuning done straight after a move tends to drift.

### Do digital pianos need humidity control?

Not in the same way. Digital pianos have no soundboard or strings, so humidity doesn't change their pitch or tuning. Keep them away from damp, direct sun, and heat sources to protect the electronics, as you would any electrical equipment.

## The short version

Buy a hygrometer, keep the reading between 40 and 50 percent, and keep it there all year. Put the piano on an interior wall away from sun, vents, and heaters. Humidify in winter, dehumidify in summer, and time your tunings for when each season has settled. Those habits prevent almost every climate problem a piano can have, and they cost far less than repairing a cracked soundboard.
