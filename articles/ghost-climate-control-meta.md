# What was wrong with the published version

1. **It blamed seasonal tuning drift on temperature.** It said heat expands the
   metal frame and raises the pitch. Seasonal drift comes from humidity: humid
   air swells the soundboard and pushes pitch sharp, dry air shrinks it and pulls
   pitch flat. Temperature on its own moves pitch far less. This is the error a
   piano technician reading the page would spot first.
2. **It said freezing moisture inside the wood can damage a cold piano.** The
   real cold-weather risks are dry winter air and condensation on the strings
   when a cold piano warms up too fast. Rewritten to say that.
3. **It recommended "a humidifier specifically designed for pianos" for the
   room.** That isn't a product category. The useful advice it missed: a room
   humidifier with a humidistat, evaporative if possible, because ultrasonic
   models filled with tap water spread mineral dust into the piano.
4. **It gave one humidity number with no source.** The new version gives the
   range with Steinway's own figures (40 to 60%, 68 to 78°F, verified on
   steinway.com) and the 42% that Dampp-Chaser systems hold.
5. **The Dampp-Chaser section had no cost or upkeep.** Now: about $650 to $900
   installed in an upright, $750 to $1,000 in a grand under 7 ft, water when the
   light comes on, pads about once a year.
