Hoffman Academy is an online piano course for children, taught on video by pianist and educator Joseph Hoffman. Its 400-plus video lessons are free, with no trial clock and no card required. Premium costs $24 a month or $239 a year and adds the practice plans, games, and sheet music that turn the videos into a complete course.

For a child between about 5 and 10, it is the strongest place to start learning piano online, and the free tier alone gets most families through the first few months. It does have one limit every parent needs to understand before starting: nothing in Hoffman Academy listens to your child play. You are the feedback loop.

How a Hoffman Academy lesson works

Each lesson is a short video, most of them 8 to 15 minutes, in which Mr. Hoffman introduces one idea and has your child try it at the keyboard. Lessons run in order through numbered units, and the site's dashboard records which ones your child has finished, so they pick up where they left off without anyone keeping track.

Hoffman recommends 15 minutes of practice a day. In practice, that means one new lesson or one review session, five or six days a week. A child who keeps that up moves through a unit in a few weeks.

The tone is pitched at young children: stories, characters, singing, and a teacher who talks to the camera the way a good primary teacher talks to a class. Six-year-olds love it. Twelve-year-olds tend to find it babyish, and that is the most reliable sign a child has aged out of it.

The method is the reason to choose it

Most piano apps teach songs. Hoffman Academy teaches musicianship, and the difference shows up within the first unit.

Children sing before they play. Hoffman uses solfège, the do-re-mi syllables, from the first lesson, so a child hears and sings a pattern, then finds it on the keys, then reads it on the staff. That order follows the Kodály tradition, and it builds an inner ear that most adult beginners never develop.

Improvisation starts in Unit 1 as well. Children make up their own variations on simple tunes like "Hot Cross Buns," often on the black keys, where nothing sounds wrong. That one habit separates children who play piano from children who reproduce pieces they've been shown.

The course also covers rhythm, note reading in both clefs, technique, theory, and ear training, in small pieces spread across the lessons. Compare that with an app like Skoove, which is excellent at getting a learner to play a recognizable song and thin on theory, ear training, and improvisation. Hoffman is the reverse: slower to deliver a pop song, much stronger on the skills underneath.

Nothing listens to your child play

Apps like Skoove, Simply Piano, and Flowkey use the device microphone or a MIDI cable to hear each note and mark it right or wrong. Hoffman Academy has no note detection. The videos teach, and the games and worksheets reinforce, but no part of the system checks whether your child played what they were asked to play.

For a young child, that's less of a loss than it sounds, because note-detecting apps can't see hand position or posture either, and those are where small children go wrong. It does mean progress depends on an adult checking in.

You don't need to read music to do that. Fifteen minutes a day, watch for these:

  1. Curved fingers. Hands shaped as if holding a small ball, with fingertips on the keys. Flat fingers are the most common habit to catch early.
  2. The right finger numbers. The video says which finger plays which note. Check that's the one your child uses.
  3. A steady beat. Clap along. If the rhythm speeds up on easy parts and stalls on hard ones, slow the whole piece down.
  4. Singing along. Hoffman asks children to sing. Children who skip it lose the part of the method that makes it work.
  5. One lesson at a time. Children like to race ahead. A lesson isn't finished until they can play it without stopping.

If you have no time for that, a note-detecting app will serve your child better, even though its curriculum is thinner.

How far Hoffman Academy goes

The course runs from absolute beginner to early intermediate. Hoffman's own song levels top out at Early Intermediate, and independent reviewers agree the course is strongest in its early units and that the later units carry less material.

For most children, that adds up to two or three years of steady practice, which is longer than many families need before deciding whether to move to a private teacher. A child who reaches the end will read in both clefs, play with both hands, understand basic harmony, and improvise, which puts them well ahead of where most app-taught beginners end up.

It won't prepare a child for graded exams or advanced repertoire. A child who reaches the later units and still wants more is ready for a teacher.

Who Hoffman Academy is for

Good fit:

  • Children from about 5 to 10 starting from zero
  • Families who want to try piano before paying for lessons
  • Homeschooling families looking for a structured music curriculum
  • Parents with no musical background who can spare 15 minutes a day to sit with their child

Poor fit:

  • Children over 11 or 12, who will find the tone too young
  • Adult beginners: Pianoforall or a feedback app like Skoove suits adults better
  • Families who can't supervise practice, and need an app that checks the notes
  • Students preparing for exams or aiming at advanced classical repertoire

What your child needs to play on

Any keyboard with full-size keys works for Hoffman Academy, because nothing needs to connect to a device. That gives you more freedom than an app does, and it means an acoustic piano works as well as a digital one.

Frequently asked questions

Is Hoffman Academy free?

Yes. All 400+ video lessons are free with no time limit and no card required. Premium costs $24 a month or $239 a year and adds guided practice sessions, seven piano games, 500+ pieces of downloadable sheet music, and printable worksheets and flashcards.

How much does Hoffman Academy Premium cost?

Premium costs $24 a month, or $239 a year, which works out to about $20 a month. Each additional family member costs $12 a month or $119 a year. Premium comes with a 30-day money-back guarantee.

What age is Hoffman Academy for?

Hoffman Academy says its lessons suit children aged 5 and up. In practice it fits children from about 5 to 10 best. Children over 11 or 12 often find the tone too young.

Is Hoffman Academy good for adults?

Adults can follow it, and it teaches solid fundamentals, but the lessons are pitched at young children. Most adult beginners do better with a course like Pianoforall or a feedback app like Skoove.

Does Hoffman Academy listen to my child play?

No. Hoffman Academy has no microphone or MIDI note detection, so it cannot tell whether your child played the right notes. A parent watching for 15 minutes a day fills that gap, and catches hand-position problems that note-detecting apps miss.

Does my child need a parent who plays piano?

No. The videos do the teaching. A parent only needs to watch for curved fingers, the right finger numbers, a steady beat, and whether the child finishes each lesson before moving on.

How long are Hoffman Academy lessons?

Most video lessons run 8 to 15 minutes. Hoffman recommends 15 minutes of practice a day, which works out to one new lesson or one review session.

How far does Hoffman Academy go?

From absolute beginner to early intermediate. A child who completes it reads music in both clefs, plays with both hands, understands basic harmony, and can improvise. For exams or advanced repertoire, the next step is a private teacher.

What is the Hoffman Academy method?

It teaches through singing, using solfège syllables (do, re, mi) from the first lesson, so children hear and sing a pattern before they play and read it. This follows the Kodály tradition. It also teaches improvisation and ear training from the start, rather than only reading and playing pieces.

Is Hoffman Academy better than Simply Piano?

For musical foundations, yes. Hoffman teaches singing, ear training, theory, and improvisation, where Simply Piano offers little. Simply Piano checks every note your child plays and gamifies practice, which suits families who can't supervise.

The verdict

Hoffman Academy gives young children the best start in piano available online, and it gives most of it away. The free videos teach musicianship rather than songs, the solfège-first method builds an ear that few app-taught beginners develop, and Premium is cheap enough to buy once you know your child is committed.

Its one real cost is your time. Nothing checks the notes, so a parent has to. Fifteen minutes a day of watching hands and clapping along is a small price for what it builds.
