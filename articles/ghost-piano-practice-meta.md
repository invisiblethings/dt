# What changed

## Cut
The original leaned on a simile in almost every section: the journey with a map,
the athlete stretching, the well-crafted symphony, the sculptor and the marble,
the tortoise and the hare, the well-balanced meal, the marathon runner. Each one
restated the point rather than adding to it. All removed.

Also gone: "working smarter, not harder", "unlocking your full musical
potential", "Believe it or not", "The truth is", "So go forth", and the closing
three paragraphs that repeated the article's own summary.

## Kept
The four-tip structure, the section order, the Liszt opening, the professionals
mantra, the Pomodoro method, all three images, and the first-person voice.

## Added
- How long to practice per day, by level, with a table. This is the highest
  volume query in the topic and the original never answered it.
- The research on why frequency beats duration, including the 2002 motor-sequence
  sleep study showing about 20 percent overnight improvement without extra practice.
- A metronome ladder with real numbers: start at 60 percent of target, +4 bpm
  after three clean runs, -8 bpm on a mistake.
- A vague-goal to specific-goal conversion table.
- A practice journal template you can copy.
- Six ways to waste a practice session.
- A ten-minute session plan for bad days.
- Ten FAQ entries with schema.
- A validated stacked bar chart showing the warm-up / focused work / cool-down
  split for 30, 45, and 60 minute sessions.

Roughly 1,700 words to roughly 2,800.

## About the chart
Inline SVG, so there is no image to host and it stays sharp at any size. The
three colours were checked for colour-blind separation and for contrast against
both light and dark backgrounds, and every segment is labelled with its minutes,
so the chart does not rely on colour alone. The table underneath repeats the same
numbers for screen readers and for AI crawlers.

# Two things to confirm

1. **The Liszt anecdote and the concert pianist framing.** Both came from the
   2022 original and I kept them. Your other posts describe you as a teacher
   rather than a performer. If the Hungarian Rhapsody story is not yours,
   replace it with one that is. A first-person claim that does not hold up is a
   liability on exactly the kind of page Google is assessing for experience.

2. **The practice-time figures** (20-30 min beginner, 45-60 intermediate, 2-4
   hours advanced) are the standard teaching ranges. Adjust them to what you tell
   your own students, since that is what makes the page yours rather than a
   restatement of every other practice article.
