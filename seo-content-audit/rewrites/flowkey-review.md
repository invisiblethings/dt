# Flowkey Review (2026): Cost, Is It Free, Pros and Cons

![Title graphic reading "Flowkey Review": a laptop on a digital piano shows Flowkey's video of a pianist's hands above sheet music](IMAGE-1)

*Last reviewed: October 2026. Prices checked on 10 October 2026.*

Flowkey is a piano app that shows you sheet music under a video of a pianist's hands. It listens to you play through a MIDI connection or your device's microphone, and it can wait for the right note before it moves on. You can use it in a web browser and on iPhone, iPad and Android.

In the US, Flowkey costs $16.49 a month or $99.99 a year for the Classic plan, which covers the courses plus classical and public-domain songs. Premium, which adds pop, rock, jazz and film music, costs $24.99 a month or $149.99 a year. Prices vary by country and by promotion, and Flowkey shows your local price on the payment page.

Part of Flowkey is free. A free account gives you a few songs and course lessons with no time limit, and the annual plans start with a 7-day free Premium trial.

My verdict is 7.2 out of 10. Flowkey suits adult beginners with some discipline and intermediate players who want to learn songs they know, and it works best with a digital piano connected by MIDI. Kids, serious classical students and anyone who wants a guided curriculum will get less from it.

I have played and taught piano for over 20 years. For this review I spent a few weeks in Flowkey with a MIDI keyboard connected, tested microphone mode, and did most of my practice on an iPad next to my digital piano.

## Pros and cons at a glance

**Pros**

- Fast, accurate feedback with a MIDI keyboard
- Wait Mode, loop, slow motion and one-hand practice
- Musical arrangements in four difficulty levels
- Works with an acoustic piano through the microphone in a quiet room
- Clean, modern interface

**Cons**

- Background noise can confuse the microphone, and it can miss mistakes
- Short courses with little depth on technique and theory
- No clear "what next" path once you finish the beginner courses
- Pop, rock and film songs need Premium, the pricier plan
- The subscription adds up if you use it for the song library alone

## Flowkey features in brief

Flowkey teaches through songs. You watch a pianist's hands on a lit-up keyboard while the sheet music scrolls underneath. The app listens through MIDI (USB or Bluetooth) or your device's microphone. Wait Mode pauses the song until you play the right notes. Other tools let you loop a section, slow the tempo, and practice one hand at a time.

Songs come in four difficulty levels: beginner, intermediate, advanced and pro. Flowkey says its library holds thousands of arrangements across classical, pop, film and TV music and other genres. Step-by-step courses cover reading sheet music, finding your way around the keyboard, chords and playing with both hands.

![Flowkey web page showing the Songs library on a tablet, with Für Elise, Imagine and Moonlight Sonata under Most Popular](IMAGE-2)

## Flowkey's strengths

With my MIDI keyboard connected, the feedback came fast and accurate. The app followed me even when I made mistakes on purpose.

Wait Mode holds the song until you play the correct note. I learned the verse of "Someone You Loved" in one sitting because Wait Mode made me get each note right before I moved on.

The arrangements sound musical, with none of the thin, robotic feel of some app versions. Four levels per song let you start with a simple version and add complexity as you improve.

![A tablet running Flowkey's video and sheet music view, propped behind a MIDI keyboard controller](IMAGE-3)

The interface looks clean and modern, one of the best-looking piano apps I have used. Courses show a completion percentage for each lesson, so you can see your progress at a glance.

## Flowkey's weak spots

![Flowkey's Microphone/MIDI settings window showing "MIDI active" with a USB keyboard connected, over the song Imagine](IMAGE-4)

- **Microphone feedback is hit or miss.** In a quiet room it worked okay. Background noise or uneven playing confused it, and several times it accepted notes I played wrong, which can build bad habits. Flowkey's own help page says the microphone works best with little background noise and a piano tuned to standard pitch (A = 440 Hz).
- **The courses stay shallow.** They suit absolute starters, but they run short and spend little time on posture, finger technique or theory. After the first lessons you land in the song library with no structured path.
- **Classical and advanced players get less.** Flowkey offers classical pieces, but most come as simplified arrangements. For full Beethoven sonatas or serious technique work, look elsewhere.
- **Finding your next song takes work.** After the intro courses, Flowkey becomes a big searchable library. That suits browsing and leaves you guessing about what to learn next.
- **Pop songs need Premium.** The Classic plan covers classical and public-domain music. "Someone You Loved" and other pop songs sit in the Premium plan.

## Flowkey pricing in 2026

Flowkey sells three paid plans, each billed per month or per year. These US prices come from Flowkey's help center, checked on 10 October 2026:

- **Classic** (courses plus classical and public-domain songs, one user): $16.49 a month, or $99.99 a year, about $8.33 a month.
- **Premium** (everything in Classic plus pop, rock, jazz, film, TV and game music, one user): $24.99 a month, or $149.99 a year, about $12.50 a month.
- **Family** (Premium for up to five profiles): $37.49 a month, or $224.99 a year.

Prices vary by country, and Flowkey runs promotions. On 10 October 2026 its home page advertised an autumn offer of "8 months free". Flowkey has no 6-month plan and no lifetime plan. You can buy Premium through the App Store or Google Play, but Classic and Family need the Flowkey web app.

Twelve months of Premium paid month by month comes to $299.88, so the annual plan cuts the cost in half if you know you will stick with it. The 7-day free trial comes with the annual plans, and discounted offers carry no trial. Seven days lets you test the core features but leaves too little time to finish a course. Flowkey also offers a 14-day money-back guarantee.

An annual plan costs less than private lessons. It costs more each year than a one-time course such as PianoForAll, which sold for $49 (list price $79) on 10 October 2026. If you practice several times a week and enjoy learning songs, the annual plan pays for itself. If you dabble, it will feel pricey.

## The players Flowkey suits

![Flowkey's Playing Scales course list showing B minor, F major and D minor lessons, each at 0% completed](IMAGE-6)

From my own use and from reading hundreds of user reviews, Flowkey works best for:

- Adult beginners and returners who already have some discipline
- People who learn best by playing songs they love
- Players with a MIDI keyboard or digital piano
- Intermediate players who want to add repertoire fast

It suits these players less:

- Total beginners who need a teacher's guidance at each step
- Kids, because the app asks for a lot of reading. My [Hoffman Academy review](https://danhon.substack.com/p/hoffman-academy-review) covers a video course built for children aged five and up.
- Players with an acoustic piano in a noisy home
- Anyone after deep classical training or improvisation

## Flowkey alternatives

I tested these side by side with Flowkey. Each fits a different goal:

- **Pianote** offers organized video lessons with teachers, including technique and theory.
- **[Simply Piano](https://www.hellosimply.com/simply-piano)** gives absolute beginners game-style lessons with the most hand-holding. It also listens through the microphone or MIDI.
- **PianoForAll** suits anyone who dislikes subscriptions: one payment ($49 on sale, list price $79) for a chord-based course with lifetime access. I cover it in my [PianoForAll review](https://danhon.substack.com/p/pianoforall-honest-review-2022).
- **tonebase Piano** focuses on classical repertoire and technique, with lessons from concert pianists and conservatory teachers.
- **Skoove** gives interactive feedback close to Flowkey's, through MIDI or the microphone. I rate it the closest runner-up.
- **Playground Sessions** teaches through game-style lessons on a MIDI keyboard. My [Playground Sessions review](https://danhon.substack.com/p/playground-sessions-review) has the details.

For me, Flowkey still wins on song-learning speed and enjoyment.

## My verdict

I give Flowkey 7.2 out of 10. It ranks among the strongest piano apps if your goal is to play songs you like and enjoy the practice. With MIDI, its feedback and its arrangements beat most of the competitors I tried.

If you get serious, you will need technique and theory lessons elsewhere. To test Flowkey, start the annual plan's 7-day trial, connect a MIDI keyboard if you can, and pick a song you love. If you still want to play after a week, the subscription makes sense. If you don't, cancel at least 24 hours before the trial ends so Flowkey doesn't charge you for the year.

I still have Flowkey on my iPad and come back to it to learn a new pop tune.

## Flowkey FAQ

### Is Flowkey free?

Flowkey has a free account with a few songs and course lessons, and you can keep it as long as you like. The full library needs a paid plan, and the annual plans start with a 7-day free Premium trial.

### How much does Flowkey cost?

In the US on 10 October 2026, Classic cost $16.49 a month or $99.99 a year, Premium $24.99 a month or $149.99 a year, and Family $37.49 a month or $224.99 a year. Prices vary by country and promotion.

### Is Flowkey good for beginners?

Yes for motivated adult beginners. Total beginners who need step-by-step guidance, and young kids, may struggle with the short courses.

### Do I need a MIDI keyboard, or does Flowkey work with an acoustic piano?

Flowkey works with an acoustic piano through your device's microphone, best in a quiet room with a tuned piano. A digital piano or keyboard connected by USB or Bluetooth MIDI gave me faster, more accurate feedback, and my [guide to beginner keyboards](https://danhon.substack.com/p/best-keyboard-piano-for-beginners) covers options if you need one.

### Can I cancel Flowkey anytime?

Yes. Flowkey says you can cancel anytime and keep access until the end of the billing period, and I cancelled twice without trouble. Cancel at least 24 hours before renewal, and if you subscribed through the App Store or Google Play, cancel there.

### Flowkey vs Simply Piano: which is better?

Simply Piano gives absolute beginners and kids more game-style hand-holding. Flowkey suits players who want to learn songs they know from sheet music and a video of a pianist's hands.

### Is Flowkey worth it long term?

If you practice and love learning songs you recognize, yes. If you dabble or want deep classical training, you may outgrow it after 6 to 12 months.
