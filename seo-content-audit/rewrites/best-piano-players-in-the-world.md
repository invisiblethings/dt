# Best Living Pianists: 7 Classical and Jazz Pianists Today

*Last reviewed: October 2026*

My picks for the best living pianists in the world today are Yuja Wang, Lang Lang, Martha Argerich, Daniil Trifonov, Hiromi, Brad Mehldau and Víkingur Ólafsson. Five of them play classical music and two play jazz. I built this list from my own listening, and the order below carries no ranking. Another critic would pick a different seven.

## My selection criteria

"Best" in music comes down to judgment, so here are the four tests I used:

- **Active now.** Each pianist is alive and performs in public as of 2026.
- **Standing with critics and juries.** I looked for competition wins, Grammy Awards and long recording careers with major labels.
- **Range.** I wanted players who cover more than one corner of the repertoire, or who move between styles.
- **Influence.** I chose pianists I hear other players and new listeners talk about, quote and copy.

Plenty of great pianists failed one test or another on the day I wrote this, and a few of them appear at the end.

## Yuja Wang (classical, born 1987)

![Yuja Wang in a teal dress, seated at a grand piano](IMAGE-1)

VIDEO-1: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ROFowmtW1eQ

Yuja Wang grew up in Beijing, studied at the Central Conservatory of Music there, and finished her training at the Curtis Institute of Music in Philadelphia with Gary Graffman. Her break came in March 2007, when she stepped in for Martha Argerich in Tchaikovsky's First Piano Concerto with the Boston Symphony Orchestra. She records for Deutsche Grammophon.

On 28 January 2023 she played all four Rachmaninoff piano concertos and the Rhapsody on a Theme of Paganini in one afternoon at Carnegie Hall, with the Philadelphia Orchestra and Yannick Nézet-Séguin. She saved the Third Concerto for last. Her concert dresses get plenty of press. I listen for her range. Teddy Abrams wrote a piano concerto for her, and their album *The American Project* (Deutsche Grammophon), with the Louisville Orchestra, won the Grammy for Best Classical Instrumental Solo in February 2024.

**Start with:** the video above, "Yuja Wang: Rachmaninoff's Third Piano Concerto", which Carnegie Hall+ published on YouTube. The title gives no performance date.

## Lang Lang (classical, born 1982)

![Lang Lang leaning on a piano keyboard, black-and-white portrait](IMAGE-2)

VIDEO-2: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s71I_EWJk7I

Lang Lang comes from Shenyang in northeast China. He started lessons at three and entered Beijing's Central Conservatory in 1991. His break came in 1999, when he replaced another soloist at a Chicago Symphony Orchestra gala and played Tchaikovsky's First Concerto. In 2008 he performed at the opening ceremony of the Beijing Olympics, and that year he set up the Lang Lang International Music Foundation to support music education for children.

He goes looking for listeners outside concert halls. In November 2021 he gave a 30-minute concert on TikTok with Deutsche Grammophon. His album *Piano Book* (Deutsche Grammophon, 2019) collects pieces students learn in their first years, from "Für Elise" to Debussy's "Clair de lune". His 2008 memoir for younger readers, *Lang Lang: Playing with Flying Keys* (Delacorte Press), written with Michael French, covers his childhood.

**Start with:** the video above, "Für Elise" performed by Lang Lang for Steinway & Sons. If you take lessons, you know this piece already.

## Martha Argerich (classical, born 1941)

![Martha Argerich seated at a grand piano, black-and-white archive photo](IMAGE-3)

VIDEO-3: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FgnE25-kvyk

Martha Argerich grew up in Buenos Aires and won the International Chopin Piano Competition in Warsaw in 1965, the first South American pianist to take the prize. She was 24.

For decades she has stayed away from solo recitals. She prefers concertos, chamber music and two-piano repertoire. She has toured with violinist Gidon Kremer and cellist Mischa Maisky, and in 2002 she launched the Progetto Martha Argerich in Lugano, a festival that put young musicians on stage beside established ones. The Berliner Philharmoniker describes Prokofiev's Third Piano Concerto as one of her favourite works for decades, and it lists her in that piece with conductor Lahav Shani in February 2027. Her 1967 studio recording of the concerto, with Claudio Abbado and the Berlin Philharmonic for Deutsche Grammophon, began her long partnership with Abbado.

**Start with:** the video above, Argerich playing Prokofiev's Third Piano Concerto with André Previn conducting. BBC television filmed her in this concerto with Previn and the London Symphony Orchestra in 1977; the upload does not say if it shows that film.

## Daniil Trifonov (classical, born 1991)

![Daniil Trifonov in a blazer, leaning on a railing beside a grand piano](IMAGE-4)

VIDEO-4: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gy5UHK4EeM8

Daniil Trifonov comes from Nizhny Novgorod in Russia and studied at the Cleveland Institute of Music with Sergei Babayan. In 2011 he won first prize and the Grand Prix at the International Tchaikovsky Competition. His recording of Liszt's Transcendental Études, *Transcendental* (Deutsche Grammophon, 2016), won the Grammy for Best Classical Instrumental Solo at the January 2018 ceremony.

He composes as well as plays. The Cleveland Institute of Music commissioned his Piano Concerto in E-flat minor, and he gave the premiere there on 23 April 2014. He played the New York premiere at Carnegie Hall on 15 November 2017, with Valery Gergiev and the Mariinsky Orchestra. On his 2020 double album *Silver Age* (Deutsche Grammophon), he pairs Scriabin's Piano Concerto with Prokofiev's Second and three movements from Stravinsky's *Petrushka*, music from the decades of Russia's Silver Age.

**Start with:** the video above, Chopin's Fantaisie-Impromptu in C-sharp minor, Op. 66, from Deutsche Grammophon. He recorded the piece for his album *Chopin Evocations* (Deutsche Grammophon, 2017).

## Hiromi (jazz, born 1979)

![Hiromi smiling at a grand piano with a keyboard on top, black-and-white photo](IMAGE-5)

VIDEO-5: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EFeouD2IWSA

Hiromi Uehara performs under her first name. She grew up in Hamamatsu, Japan. A childhood piano teacher introduced her to jazz, and in 1999 she moved to Boston to study at Berklee College of Music. Telarc signed her before she graduated, and Ahmad Jamal co-produced her debut, *Another Mind* (Telarc, 2003), which sold 100,000 copies in Japan.

She has recorded with Chick Corea and Stanley Clarke. Her current quartet, Hiromi's Sonicwonder, has Adam O'Farrill on trumpet, Hadrien Feraud on bass and Gene Coye on drums, and the band released *Sonicwonderland* on Telarc on 6 October 2023. In her solos I hear blues phrases, classical runs and rock riffs in the space of a minute, and she plays all of them at speed.

**Start with:** the video above, "Desire" by The Trio Project, her band with bassist Anthony Jackson and drummer Simon Phillips. The studio version is on their album *Voice* (Telarc, 2011).

## Brad Mehldau (jazz, born 1970)

![Brad Mehldau playing a Steinway grand piano on stage](IMAGE-6)

VIDEO-6: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kruKQCY77bc

Brad Mehldau comes from Jacksonville, Florida, and moved to New York in 1988 to study at The New School. In 1995 he formed his trio with bassist Larry Grenadier and drummer Jorge Rossy. He plays jazz standards beside rock and pop songs, and his solo work leans on classical forms: *After Bach* (Nonesuch, 2018) answers pieces from Bach's Well-Tempered Clavier with his own.

His album *Finding Gabriel* (Nonesuch, 2019) won the Grammy for Best Jazz Instrumental Album at the 2020 ceremony, his first Grammy. In April 2020, at home in Amsterdam during lockdown, he wrote the twelve solo pieces of *Suite: April 2020*, which Nonesuch released that June. He returned to the Beatles in 2023 with *Your Mother Should Know: Brad Mehldau Plays The Beatles* (Nonesuch), a live solo set from the Philharmonie de Paris.

**Start with:** the video above, Mehldau playing the Beatles' "Blackbird" for Steinway & Sons. He first recorded the song on *The Art of the Trio, Volume One* (Warner Bros., 1997).

## Víkingur Ólafsson (classical, born 1984)

![Víkingur Ólafsson on a concert stage at a grand piano, gesturing toward the audience](IMAGE-7)

VIDEO-7: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h3-rNMhIyuQ

Víkingur Ólafsson grew up in Reykjavík, where his mother, a piano teacher, gave him his first lessons. He took his degrees at the Juilliard School in New York. He signed with Deutsche Grammophon in 2016, and his first album for the label, *Philip Glass: Piano Works*, came out in January 2017 for Glass's 80th birthday.

His albums build programmes around an idea. *Mozart & Contemporaries* (2021) sets Mozart beside Haydn, C.P.E. Bach, Galuppi and Cimarosa. He spent the 2023–24 season touring Bach's Goldberg Variations across six continents, and his recording (Deutsche Grammophon, October 2023) won the Grammy for Best Classical Instrumental Solo in February 2025, his first.

**Start with:** the video above, the Andante from Bach's Organ Sonata No. 4, BWV 528, in August Stradal's piano transcription. It comes from his album *Johann Sebastian Bach* (Deutsche Grammophon, 2018).

## Honorable mentions

- **Igor Levit** (classical). On 12 March 2020, as lockdowns began, he started streaming "house concerts" from his flat on Twitter, playing music from Beethoven to Morton Feldman.
- **Khatia Buniatishvili** (classical, born 1987). The Georgian pianist signed with Sony Classical in 2010, and her first album for the label, a Liszt recital, marked the 200th anniversary of Liszt's birth in 2011.
- **Joey Alexander** (jazz, born 2003). He grew up in Bali, taught himself jazz by ear from his father's records, and at 12 earned two nominations at the 2016 Grammy Awards, the first Indonesian musician nominated.
