# Piano Maintenance Guide: Tuning, Care and Costs (2026)

![Title graphic reading "Caring for Your Piano Tuning Essentials" over a blurred, warm-toned image of a man in a waistcoat working at an old upright piano](IMAGE-1)

*Last reviewed: October 2026. Prices checked on 10 October 2026.*

Tune an acoustic piano at least twice a year once it has passed its first year. Baldwin, Kawai and Yamaha give that figure in the manufacturer advice the Piano Technicians Guild (PTG) collects on its piano care page, and Steinway's UK care guide (March 2023) says the same.

In the US, Thumbtack's cost guide (updated 20 October 2025) puts a standard tuning at $121 to $231, with a national average of $168. Two tunings a year come to about $240 to $460. Pitch raises, regulation, voicing and repairs cost extra. These are 2025 US figures; prices vary by region and technician, so get local quotes.

You control the two things that decide how long a tuning holds: the humidity in the room and the spot where the piano stands. PTG gives 68°F (20°C) and 42% relative humidity as the target, with the piano away from windows, outside doors, vents and direct sun.

## Tuning schedule by situation

- **New piano.** A new piano settles in its first year. On PTG's page, Baldwin cites the National Piano Manufacturers Association's advice of four tunings in the first year. Yamaha's global care guide asks for two; the Yamaha section on PTG's page says four. Plan on two to four, and ask your dealer.
- **Home piano.** Twice a year after the first year, per Baldwin, Kawai, Yamaha and Steinway UK. Young Chang and Yamaha's global care guide set a floor of once a year, and the Steinway section on PTG's page asks for three or four visits a year. Consumers' Checkbook says most experts recommend once or twice a year, often spring and fall.
- **Teaching, practice-room and heavy-use pianos.** PTG notes that constant playing wears a tuning down faster, and Schimmel says a piano played often and hard may need extra tunings.
- **Concert and studio pianos.** Checkbook reports that halls and recording studios tune before each performance or session.

PTG's rule of thumb: tune often enough to keep the note A between 439 and 441 Hz.

## Signs your piano needs tuning

- Chords sound sour, or a single note wavers instead of ringing clean.
- The piano sounds flat against a recording, a digital keyboard or another instrument.
- Six months have passed, or the season has turned from humid to dry.
- You moved the piano.

## Inside a tuning visit

A technician sets the tension of each string by turning its tuning pin with a tuning lever, working by ear, with an electronic tuning device, or with both. The reference pitch is A = 440 Hz.

Many technicians also check the keys, action and pedals. Thumbtack notes that some include small fixes such as a sticking key in the price and others charge extra, so ask what the fee covers. Mention any squeaking pedal when you book; the [guide to piano pedals](https://danhon.substack.com/p/piano-pedals) explains what each pedal does.

Thumbtack gives these visit lengths:

- A piano tuned on a regular schedule: one to one and a half hours.
- A piano that has gone about two years without a tuning, or has moved: about two hours.
- A neglected piano: two and a half to three hours.

A piano left untuned for a long time drops below A440. Kawai says it then needs a pitch raise, an extra pass that brings all the strings up close to pitch, before a fine tuning will hold. Some pianos need more than one, and some old pianos can no longer carry the string tension A440 requires, Kawai notes.

## Piano maintenance costs per year

These US national figures come from Thumbtack's cost guides for piano tuning (updated 20 October 2025) and piano moving (updated 15 October 2025), based on estimates its users received from local professionals. Your area may run higher or lower.

- **Tuning:** $121 to $231 a visit, national average $168. Two visits a year: about $240 to $460.
- **Pitch raise ("pitch correction"):** about $60 on top of the tuning.
- **Technician's rate for extra work:** about $100 an hour, up to $200 for large grands.
- **Regulation:** $200 to $600 on average. Full action reconditioning and regulation runs $800 to $2,500 for an upright and $2,500 to $3,500 for a grand.
- **Voicing:** $175 to $600.
- **Minor repairs:** about $65 an hour, plus parts. Ask in advance who pays if a string breaks during a tuning.
- **Humidity-control system fitted inside the piano:** $450 to $800 installed.
- **Moving a piano:** $194 to $448, national average $294. Stairs, distance and narrow doorways add to the price.

You pay for regulation and voicing at longer intervals. Steinway UK recommends a service after the first year and then once each three to five years, and Checkbook gives three to five years for voicing.

Get at least two quotes. In a Consumers' Checkbook price survey on one 10-year-old Steinway grand, the highest quote came to more than three times the lowest. The [piano vs guitar comparison](https://danhon.substack.com/p/piano-vs-guitar-which-is-easier) sets these upkeep costs against a guitar's if you have not yet chosen an instrument.

## Humidity, temperature and placement

A piano's wooden soundboard swells in damp air and shrinks in dry air, which changes string tension and pulls the piano out of tune, Checkbook explains. Steinway UK says large swings can also crack the soundboard, and Thumbtack warns that damp can rust strings.

The two main sources give different targets:

- **PTG:** 68°F (20°C) and 42% relative humidity.
- **Steinway UK:** 20°C and between 45% and 70% relative humidity, with conditions kept steady.

Put a hygrometer and a thermometer in the room. Run a humidifier in dry months and a dehumidifier or air conditioner in damp ones.

Placement advice from PTG and Steinway UK:

- Set the piano near a wall, away from windows and doors you open often.
- Keep it clear of heating and air-conditioning vents, radiators, fireplaces and direct sunlight.
- Keep picture frames and other objects off the lid; they can mark the finish.

A technician can also fit a humidity-control system inside the piano, such as Dampp-Chaser's Piano Life Saver System, which humidifies and dehumidifies under a controller. After a move, Thumbtack suggests waiting until the piano has sat in its new room for three months before you pay for a tuning, because the change of climate unsettles it.

## Cleaning the case and keys

Steinway UK's care guide covers the safe jobs you can do yourself:

- Dust the case with a soft cloth, and use a microfibre cloth on polished surfaces.
- For dirt, use a cloth with a mild detergent diluted in water, and test a small hidden spot first.
- Wipe the keys with a cloth dampened with a little water. Keep water from running down between the keys.
- Skip solvents on the keys, and skip alcohol, abrasive cleaners, wax and furniture polish on the case.

## Jobs to leave to a technician

- **Tuning, pitch raises, regulation and voicing.** Steinway wants a professional, and no one else, to tune its pianos.
- **Repairs.** Steinway UK recommends trained technicians and genuine parts.
- **Cleaning inside the piano**, including dust buildup and pests such as moths or silverfish (Steinway UK).
- **Moving.** Thumbtack warns that you can damage the piano, and your back, without proper equipment and helpers.

## Strings per note: one, two or three

A modern piano has 88 keys and around 230 strings, with the exact count depending on the model, according to Yamaha. Each tenor and treble key strikes three strings. Toward the bass, the count drops from three strings to two and then to one for the lowest notes, and copper wire wraps the bass strings.

Yamaha's guide shows that small pitch differences between the strings of one note change how it rings. If one string drifts, the whole note can sound off.

## Finding a qualified piano technician

- **US and Canada:** PTG's Find an RPT search takes a US ZIP code or a Canadian postal code. A Registered Piano Technician (RPT) has passed PTG's exams on maintenance, repair and tuning. Checkbook reports that more than half of those who sit the exams fail, and it adds that some skilled tuners hold no RPT credential.
- **UK:** The Pianoforte Tuners' Association, founded in 1913, has a "Find a tuner" search by postal town on its website.

Piano teachers and music shops can name the technicians they use. Before you book, ask about credentials, what the fee includes and travel charges.

## Digital pianos and keyboards

A digital piano has no strings, so it needs no tuning. Many models, the Clavinova among them, have a pitch control for matching other instruments or a recording.

Yamaha's Clavinova manual (CLP-155) lists the upkeep a digital piano needs:

- Keep it away from direct sunlight, high humidity and heat.
- Close the key cover or use the dust cover after you play, and switch the power off.
- Clean the cabinet and keys with a clean cloth with a little moisture in it, plus a neutral cleanser if you like. Abrasive cleansers, waxes, solvents and chemical dust cloths can dull the finish.

## Piano maintenance FAQ

### How often should you tune a piano?

At least twice a year after the first year, per Baldwin, Kawai, Yamaha and Steinway UK. Plan on two to four tunings in a new piano's first year, and more for a piano in heavy use.

### How much does piano maintenance cost per year?

In the US, two tunings at Thumbtack's 2025 range of $121 to $231 each come to about $240 to $460 a year, plus about $60 for any pitch raise. Regulation and voicing come due once in three to five years. Prices vary by region and technician.

### Can I tune my piano myself?

Leave tuning to a professional; Steinway gives the same advice. You can handle dusting, cleaning the keys and controlling humidity yourself.

### Do digital pianos need tuning?

No. A digital piano has no strings. Keep it out of direct sun, heat and damp, and cover it after use.
