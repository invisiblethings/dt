<h2>Want to Learn Jazz Piano? Start with These Tried-and-Tested Books</h2>
<p>If you’re searching for the best way to learn jazz piano—whether you&#8217;re a complete beginner or brushing off the keys after years away—these jazz piano books offer some of the most helpful, inspiring, and practical approaches available. From swing and blues progressions to left-hand voicings and improvisation techniques, each book on this list brings something valuable to the table. And while some require basic or intermediate sight-reading skills, the payoff is well worth it.</p>
<h2>Recommended Jazz Piano Books</h2>
<h2>The Jazz Piano Method – by Jean Robur</h2>
<p><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Jazz-piano-method.jpg" alt="Jazz piano method jean robur" width="148" height="200" />Level: Beginner to Advanced (basic sight-reading required)<br />
This book is worth buying for the jazz chord progressions alone. It starts off gently, easing you in like a beginner, then suddenly throws you into the deep end with more advanced concepts. Don’t let that put you off—it’s a book you’ll return to many times over the years as your playing improves.</p>
<h2>Jazz Keyboard Toolbox – by Bill Cunliffe</h2>
<p><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Bill-Cunliffe-Jazz-keyboard-Toolbox.jpg" alt="jazz keyboard toolbox - Bill Cunliffe" width="151" height="201" />Level: Beginner to Intermediate<br />
This is a fantastic book for learning three-note left-hand voicings, which are essential in jazz. It includes simple yet effective blues and jazz pieces that sound great without being overwhelming. Great for developing both technique and musicality.</p>
<h2>Exploring Jazz Piano &amp; Exploring Blues Piano – by Tim Richards</h2>
<p><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Exploring-Jazz-piano-tim-richards.jpg" alt="Exploring Jazz piano tim richards" width="161" height="200" /><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Blues-Piano-Tim-Richards.jpg" alt="Blues Piano Tim Richards" width="153" height="200" />Level: Intermediate (solid sight-reading recommended)<br />
These two books are practically mini-courses in themselves. They offer a structured, step-by-step approach to jazz piano, complete with exercises, theory, and listening suggestions. Ideal if you&#8217;re looking to deepen your understanding of jazz harmony and develop improvisational skills.</p>
<h2>Cool Jazz – from the Jazz Piano Solos Series</h2>
<p><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Cool-jazz.jpg" alt="Cool Jazz" width="150" height="200" />Level: Intermediate<br />
Features stylish arrangements of classics like So What, Blue in Green, and a playable version of Take Five. These are interpretations—not transcriptions—but they capture the feel of cool jazz piano beautifully. A fun book to explore once you&#8217;re comfortable with chords and jazz rhythms.</p>
<h2>Blues Hanon / Jazz Hanon</h2>
<p><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Jazz-Hanon.jpg" alt="" width="151" height="201" /><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Blues-Hanon.jpg" alt="Blues Hanon" width="146" height="200" />Level: Beginner to Intermediate<br />
These might look like old-school exercises, but don’t judge the book by its cover. The patterns are surprisingly useful for building jazz technique and finger independence, all while keeping things rooted in blues and jazz flavor. Great for warming up or working on speed and agility.</p>
