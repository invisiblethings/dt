If you've searched for a single number, here's the honest answer: most localized roof repairs in Brooklyn run **$350 to $1,500**, with the wide range coming down to three things — what's actually wrong, how hard it is to access, and what your roof is made of. Here's how that breaks down by situation.

## Typical Repair Costs by Type

**Flashing repair (chimney, vent, skylight):** $300–$800. Flashing is thin metal sealing the joint between your roof and anything that pokes through it. It fails before the surrounding roofing material does, and it's one of the most common single-source leak causes we find.

**Missing or damaged shingles (small section):** $300–$900. Cost depends on how closely we need to match your existing shingle color and profile, and whether the underlayment beneath the damaged shingles also needs replacing.

**Flat roof membrane repair:** $400–$1,800. A small seam reseal is at the low end; a larger patch addressing blistering or a soft deck underneath runs higher.

**Active leak diagnosis and repair:** $350–$1,200. This includes the diagnostic time to trace the water path — often the largest cost driver isn't materials, it's the labor to find where water is actually entering versus where it's showing up inside.

**Gutter repair or resealing:** $150–$600, often bundled with a roof repair visit since gutter problems and roof problems frequently share a cause.

## What Pushes the Price Up

**Roof access.** A steep pitch, a roof with no easy ladder access, or a shared party-wall building that needs coordinated scaffolding all add labor time before any actual repair work starts.

**Roof deck damage.** If water has been getting in long enough to rot the wood decking underneath the roofing material, that decking has to be replaced before anything else can be repaired properly — patching over rotted decking just fails again.

**Material age and availability.** Matching a 15-year-old shingle color that's been discontinued, or sourcing a specific membrane type for an older flat roof, sometimes costs more than working with current, readily available materials.

**Multiple, unrelated problem areas.** A roof with one clear failure point is a straightforward repair. A roof with several separate issues (age-related wear here, storm damage there, an unrelated flashing failure somewhere else) starts to approach the point where a broader repair — or a conversation about replacement — makes more financial sense than three separate small jobs.

## What Keeps the Price Down

Getting to a leak early, before it causes secondary damage to decking or interior finishes, is by far the biggest lever homeowners actually control. A $400 flashing repair caught this year is a $1,200+ job — decking replacement included — if it's left for two more rainy seasons.
