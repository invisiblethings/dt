Whole-Home & Gut Renovations

Scope of work

A whole-home renovation is a coordination job. The tile setter can't start until the plumber passes rough inspection, and the painter can't start until the drywall is taped and sanded. As the general contractor, we own that sequence, run the schedule, and stay your one point of contact from demo to the final walkthrough.

We take on interior renovations of houses, two-families and apartments: wall removals and new layouts, new drywall, electrical and plumbing upgrades by licensed trades, kitchens, bathrooms, floors, doors and trim, and paint. Owners who've used us for a renovation mention that the price was fair and the process was smooth, and that's the standard we hold ourselves to.

What's included

  • Planning, scope and written schedule
  • Permit coordination with your architect or engineer where required
  • Demolition and abatement coordination where required
  • Framing, wall removal with engineered beams, and new layouts
  • Licensed electrical and plumbing upgrades
  • Insulation, drywall, plaster repair and skim-coating
  • Kitchens, bathrooms, flooring, doors, trim and millwork
  • Painting, punch list and final cleaning
02S-206

Local conditions

Renovating Staten Island's housing stock

Every era of house. Staten Island has 1890s Victorians on the North Shore, mid-century capes and ranches across the middle of the island, and 1980s–2000s attached and semi-attached two-families in the south. Each has its own predictable surprises behind the walls. We price contingencies honestly instead of hiding them.

Removing walls. Opening a kitchen to a living room often means a load-bearing wall. That requires a licensed engineer or architect and a DOB filing, and we build to their drawings.

Renovating with tenants or family next door. In two-family homes, we plan utility shutoffs, noise and dust containment around the occupied unit.

FAQ

Home renovations questions

Do you work with my architect?

Yes. When the job needs drawings and DOB filings, we build to your architect's or engineer's plans. If you don't have one, we can recommend professionals we've worked with.

Can we live in the house during the renovation?

For phased projects, often yes. We set up dust walls, keep one bathroom working where possible, and sequence the work by area. Full gut renovations usually go faster and cleaner if the house is empty.

How do you handle surprises behind the walls?

We put realistic allowances in the quote for the kind of house you have, document anything we find with photos, and price changes in writing before doing the work.
